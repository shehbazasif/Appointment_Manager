RantevouOS — MVP Product Requirements Document
Greece-first Appointment Management SaaS | Build Specification for Codex / GitHub Copilot
Document status: MVP build-ready specification
Target market: Greece
Initial vertical: salons, barbers, beauty studios, nail studios, spas and other appointment-based SMEs
Primary product goal: working demo product that can later evolve into a commercial multi-tenant SaaS.
1. Executive Summary
RantevouOS is a Greece-first, web-based appointment management SaaS for appointment-based small businesses. The MVP must allow a business owner to configure their business, services, staff and working hours; publish a public booking page; accept customer appointments; manage them in a calendar; maintain a customer CRM; and send appointment notifications by email. SMS is an optional future integration and must be simulated in the demo rather than requiring paid SMS. WhatsApp is explicitly out of scope for the MVP and must not be required for core functionality.
The MVP should be intentionally simple to deploy and operate. Use the latest stable Nuxt 4.x release available at implementation time, Vue 3, TypeScript, Nitro server APIs, PostgreSQL, Drizzle ORM and PrimeVue. Do not introduce microservices, Kubernetes, Kafka, RabbitMQ or a paid managed database for the MVP.
2. Product Vision
Positioning: 'Your business calendar, customers and reminders — in one place.' Long-term positioning: a WhatsApp-first operating system for appointment businesses in Greece.
The product should eventually expand into WhatsApp messaging, automated rebooking, marketing automation, payments, multi-location management, AI receptionist and voice calling. These future capabilities must be considered in domain boundaries and database design but must not complicate the MVP.
3. MVP Objectives
•	Create a realistic demo that can be shown to Greek salons and appointment businesses.
•	Prove the complete booking lifecycle from public booking page to business calendar and customer CRM.
•	Use a low-cost/open-source-first architecture that can run on a VPS with self-hosted PostgreSQL.
•	Keep the architecture modular so future notification, WhatsApp, billing and other services can be extracted without rewriting the core domain.
•	Minimize custom UI work by using PrimeVue and established Vue libraries.
4. Users and Roles
•	SUPER_ADMIN: platform owner. Manages businesses and system/demo data. Basic MVP implementation only.
•	OWNER: business owner. Full access to the business, calendar, appointments, customers, staff, services, settings and billing placeholder.
•	MANAGER: manages day-to-day operations but cannot manage subscription/billing or platform settings.
•	STAFF: sees and manages appointments assigned to them and limited customer information.
•	CUSTOMER: public booking user. No account is required in MVP; name, phone and email are collected at booking.
5. Target Business and Example
Initial demo business: Maria Beauty Studio, Athens, Greece. Seed realistic data: 5 staff members, 8–12 services, at least 100 customers and several months of appointments. The demo must look populated and usable without requiring manual setup.
6. Core User Journeys
6.1 Business Onboarding
1.	Register with email and password.
2.	Create organization/business: name, slug, description, phone, email, address, city, country, timezone and currency.
3.	Add services with name, duration, price and active/inactive status.
4.	Add staff members and assign services.
5.	Configure business working hours and optional staff-specific hours.
6.	Publish/activate public booking page.
7.	Configure email notification settings.
8.	Show onboarding completion and public booking URL.
6.2 Customer Booking
9.	Customer opens /book/{businessSlug}.
10.	Selects service.
11.	Optionally selects staff or chooses 'Any available'.
12.	Selects date.
13.	System calculates available slots from business hours, staff availability, blocked times, existing appointments and service duration.
14.	Customer selects a slot.
15.	Customer enters name, phone and email.
16.	System validates availability again server-side.
17.	Create appointment atomically to prevent double booking.
18.	Show confirmation page and send confirmation email.
19.	Customer does not need to create an account.
6.3 Appointment Management
20.	Owner/staff can create an appointment manually.
21.	Appointment can be confirmed, cancelled, rescheduled, checked-in and completed.
22.	Support NO_SHOW.
23.	Record status history.
24.	Rescheduling must re-check availability.
25.	Cancellation must release the time slot.
26.	Completed appointments remain in customer history.
6.4 Reminder Flow
MVP notification lifecycle:
•	Immediately after confirmation: appointment confirmation email.
•	24 hours before appointment: reminder email.
•	After completion: optional thank-you/review-request email.
•	Notification jobs and delivery status must be stored in the database.
•	SMS must have a 'Preview/Simulate Send' mode; do not call a paid SMS API in the demo.
7. Functional Requirements
7.1 Authentication
•	Email/password registration and login.
•	Secure password hashing using Argon2id or another modern password hashing implementation.
•	Secure session-based authentication; do not store raw passwords.
•	Logout.
•	Protected dashboard routes.
•	Role-based authorization.
•	Organization/tenant must be attached to authenticated membership.
7.2 Multi-Tenancy
The application is multi-tenant from day one. Every business belongs to an organization. Business-owned records must contain organization_id directly or be safely reachable through an organization-owned parent.
•	Never trust organization_id supplied by the browser.
•	Resolve the active organization from the authenticated membership/session.
•	Every protected query must enforce tenant isolation.
•	Add tests proving Business A cannot access Business B data.
•	Use a reusable authorization/data-access layer to reduce the chance of tenant leakage.
7.3 Business Settings
•	Business name, logo placeholder, description, phone, email, address, city, country.
•	Default timezone: Europe/Athens for Greek demo businesses.

7.5 Staff

11. Database Strategy
Use PostgreSQL from the beginning. For the demo, self-host PostgreSQL in Docker on a VPS or local development machine. Do not depend on a paid managed database. The application must be portable to any standard PostgreSQL server.
Supabase Free and Cloudflare D1 can be useful for experiments, but they are not the primary database strategy for this project. Avoid building business logic that depends on a provider-specific database feature.

13. Appointment Status Model
Allowed states: PENDING, CONFIRMED, CHECKED_IN, COMPLETED, CANCELLED, NO_SHOW, RESCHEDULED. Store status transitions in appointment_status_history. Do not hard-delete completed appointment history.
14. Availability Algorithm
27.	Determine business timezone.
28.	Determine requested date in business timezone.
29.	Check business hours.
30.	Check staff hours.
31.	Check staff/service assignment.
32.	Remove blocked periods.
33.	Remove existing appointments that overlap the requested interval.
34.	Generate slots according to a configurable slot interval, default 15 minutes.
35.	Return only slots that fit the full service duration.
36.	On booking POST, repeat the availability check server-side inside a database transaction.
37.	Prevent overlapping appointments using a PostgreSQL-safe strategy. Prefer a database constraint/exclusion strategy where practical; otherwise use transactional locking with a final overlap query.

16. API Rules
•	All request bodies validated with Zod or equivalent schema validation.
•	Return consistent JSON error format.
•	Never trust client-provided organization_id.
•	Use HTTP status codes correctly.
•	Protect private endpoints with auth middleware.
•	Public booking endpoint must be rate-limited.
•	Do not expose internal database errors to customers.
•	Use server-side date/time normalization.
17. Security
•	Argon2id password hashing.
•	Secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookies for sessions.
•	CSRF protection where applicable.
•	Rate limiting for login and public booking.
•	Input validation on every mutation.
•	Tenant isolation tests.
•	Authorization checks on every protected resource.
•	Audit important administrative actions.
•	Do not log passwords, session tokens or full private customer data.
•	Use environment variables for secrets.
•	HTTPS in production.
•	Prepare architecture for GDPR requirements: data export, deletion, retention and consent records.
18. Email Architecture
Create an EmailService interface so the rest of the application never directly depends on Resend/Brevo SDK calls.
Example conceptual interface: sendAppointmentConfirmation(), sendAppointmentReminder(), sendCancellation(), sendReschedule(), sendReviewRequest(), sendTestEmail(). Provider selection comes from environment configuration.
For the demo, use a verified sending domain if the provider requires it. Do not ask business owners for their Gmail password. Gmail should only be integrated later via OAuth if Google Calendar/Gmail integration becomes a requirement.
19. SMS Architecture
SMS is not required for MVP. Build SmsService as an interface with a MockSmsProvider. In demo mode, clicking Send SMS creates a log and shows the message preview without sending a paid SMS. Later implement Twilio or another provider without changing appointment business logic.
20. WhatsApp — Explicitly Out of Scope for MVP
Do not implement WhatsApp API, unofficial WhatsApp libraries, QR-based WhatsApp sessions or WhatsApp automation in MVP. Keep a NotificationChannel abstraction so WhatsApp can be added later through the official Meta WhatsApp Business Platform.
21. Future Architecture
MVP: Nuxt 4 + Nitro + Drizzle + PostgreSQL. Later add Redis for caching/queues. When traffic or operational requirements justify it, extract domains such as messaging, notifications, billing or AI into independent services. Do not start with microservices.

34. Build Directive
Build the MVP as a production-quality demo, not a throwaway prototype. However, do not over-engineer it. Prioritize correctness of tenant isolation, availability, appointment transactions, clean domain boundaries and a polished PrimeVue-based UI. All MVP features must work without WhatsApp, paid SMS or a paid managed database.
When a third-party provider is optional, implement an interface and a mock/local provider first. When a feature is marked future/out-of-scope, create only the abstraction needed to avoid future rewrites; do not implement the feature.
The final MVP should be runnable with one command after environment setup, document required environment variables, include database migrations and seed data, and include automated tests for the critical booking path.

Current vendor plans/pricing can change and should be rechecked before commercial launch. This PRD intentionally avoids hard-coding third-party pricing into the product.
